# P4A / Cyber-Republic notes from the contractor-readiness work

These are separate design notes for `p4a-xyz-cinema`. They do not change that site and they do not turn a civic or referendum process into a government procurement process.

## Better

1. Add an evidence boundary to every public claim: proposed, source-backed, modelled, tested, adopted or delivered.
2. Give each civic module an accountable vehicle: private citizens, unincorporated group, incorporated body, company, research partner, government body or not yet decided.
3. Show who may decide, who may advise and who may only contribute evidence.
4. Use a public requirements matrix for any future funded work: source, rule, owner, evidence, status and verifier.
5. Keep constitutional legitimacy separate from contractor capability. A capable software or research supplier does not gain democratic authority by winning work.

## Faster

1. Reuse the tender lab's source register, requirements matrix, decision gate and public/private split.
2. Let LLMs extract clauses, compare versions, maintain questions and draft plain-English explanations.
3. Keep one human gate for source confirmation, one for rights and risk, and one for publication.
4. Build small simulations from versioned public data before commissioning a large platform.
5. Record why an option was rejected so later agents do not restart the same debate.

## Fairer

1. Publish the participation rules before collecting submissions.
2. Provide plain English, accessible formats and low-bandwidth downloads.
3. Separate consultation, deliberation, voting, procurement and implementation. Each needs different authority and safeguards.
4. Declare conflicts, funding, vendors, model limits, changes and correction pathways.
5. Avoid pay-to-participate or vendor-lock-in pathways.
6. Give First Nations governance, Country and cultural authority their own decision lane. Procurement preference is not cultural permission.
7. Make minority reports, dissent and unresolved questions visible rather than forcing false consensus.

## Practical page additions to consider later

- A `Who can decide what?` panel on the Cyber-Republic page.
- A versioned referendum-question register with source, drafter, legal status and unresolved consequences.
- A simulation receipt that records assumptions, model version, public inputs and known omissions.
- A supplier and research-partner register if the simulator ever receives funding.
- A no-black-box rule: an LLM may explain and compare, but may not silently set the constitutional options, weights or verdict.

## Useful bridge from Windemere

The Windemere tender workspace already demonstrates a strong civic pattern: public source documents, a compliance matrix, role boundaries, decision gates, private pricing and specialist review. The Cyber-Republic can borrow that transparent working method without pretending a referendum is a tender.

## Useful bridge from Gumpi, Sandworm and Grain by Grain

Use a visible stage ladder so a large public idea cannot jump from imagination to implied mandate:

1. `proposed`: a citizen or team publishes the idea and its assumptions.
2. `researching`: evidence, options, constraints and affected authorities are mapped.
3. `simulated`: costs, routes, impacts and alternatives are modelled with stated limits.
4. `deliberating`: community, Traditional Owner, technical and government views are recorded without forcing consensus.
5. `authorised`: the body with legal or cultural authority makes the relevant decision.
6. `procuring`: an accountable buyer runs a separate, fair supplier process.
7. `delivering`: the contracted entities report against scope, money, outcomes and changes.

The referendum or civic process decides direction. It must not secretly choose Luke's company, Sandworm, Aura or any other supplier. Those entities compete or partner through a separately governed procurement lane.
